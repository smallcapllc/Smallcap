In small-cap private investing, the best opportunities rarely arrive as polished teasers in an inbox. They surface through relationships, quiet conversations, and a reputation for being a serious, discreet buyer. Auction processes have their place, but they often compress diligence, inflate prices, and favor the bidder with the most aggressive assumptions—not the partner best suited to steward the business.

Precision investing starts earlier: with how you find the conversation in the first place.

## Why proprietary flow compounds

When a deal is broadly shopped, you compete on price and speed. When a deal reaches you first—or only—you compete on fit, trust, and structure. That difference shows up in entry valuation, in the quality of information you receive before signing a letter of intent, and in whether the seller still wants to work with you after closing.

Proprietary flow is not a slogan. It is a system: a set of habits that make you the person founders, advisors, and intermediaries call when a situation is delicate, off-market, or simply not ready for a formal process.

## Build a network that feeds itself

A useful network in this market is not a long contact list. It is a smaller circle of people who understand what you buy, what you will not buy, and how you behave under pressure.

Prioritize relationships with:

- Accountants and fractional CFOs who serve owner-operated companies
- Commercial bankers who know when a credit line or estate plan is forcing a conversation
- Attorneys who handle succession, buy-sell agreements, and estate matters
- Industry operators who hear about competitors considering a sale long before a banker is hired
- Other investors who pass on deals outside their mandate but inside yours

The discipline is reciprocity. Share useful introductions. Close the loop when someone sends you a lead, even when the answer is no. Be clear about your criteria so you do not waste their credibility.

## Be specific about what you want

Vague mandates produce vague referrals. "We look at interesting small businesses" is not a filter anyone can act on. A usable mandate sounds more like: durable cash flow, a defensible niche, a leadership team that can stay or be supported, and a seller who cares about continuity—not only the last dollar.

Write that mandate down. Share it with your closest sources. Update it when your thesis evolves. Specificity is a form of respect: it tells partners you will not send every teaser into a black hole.

## Create reasons for people to call you

Owners and advisors call buyers they already trust. Trust is built in public and private ways:

- Publish clear thinking about how you evaluate businesses (without turning every post into a pitch)
- Show up consistently in the industries you claim to understand
- Complete diligence thoroughly when you engage—and walk away cleanly when you should
- Treat confidential information as confidential, every time

Your brand in this market is not a logo. It is the pattern of how you treat people when a deal is live and when it is not.

## Use intermediaries without becoming dependent on them

Bankers and brokers remain important. Many excellent businesses will only sell through a process. The mistake is waiting for processes to find you.

Engage intermediaries early as relationships, not only as recipients of your "buyer profile" PDF. Ask what they are seeing in a sector. Be a reliable first call for situations that are too small, too operationally complex, or too relationship-sensitive for a broad auction. When you do participate in a process, be on time, be prepared, and do not re-trade lightly. Reputation travels.

## Turn research into inbound

Thesis-driven research is underused in lower middle market work. Choose a narrow industry. Map the companies. Learn who owns them, who is aging out of the business, who has no clear successor, and who has quietly professionalized the operation. Then approach with a point of view—not a cold "are you for sale?"

Owners respond to buyers who have done the homework. That is not flattery. It is evidence that you will not waste their time.

## Measure the right leading indicators

Deal flow quality is easier to feel than to measure, but a few leading indicators help:

- Share of conversations that start off-market
- Number of warm introductions per quarter from your core network
- Ratio of first meetings to signed NDAs to serious diligence
- How often sellers or advisors invite you back into a later process

Vanity metrics—total teasers received—mostly measure how widely your email is distributed.

## Preservation first, even in sourcing

Chasing volume invites noise. Noise invites rushed judgment. Rushed judgment is how capital gets permanently impaired.

A preservation-first posture in sourcing means you would rather miss a deal than force one. It means your calendar has room for deep diligence. It means saying no early so you can say yes with conviction later.

Alpha in small-cap markets often lives where institutional attention is thin. Finding those situations is less about speed and more about being known, trusted, and precise—long before a CIM exists.
